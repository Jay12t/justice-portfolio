Electromechanical energy conversion: the big picture

Motoring action

Electrical input is converted into mechanical shaft output plus losses. In a DC motor, armature current in a magnetic field produces force and torque. In AC machines, rotating magnetic fields interact with rotor currents or magnetic fields to produce torque.

Generating action

Mechanical input turns a conductor or magnetic system so electromagnetic induction produces electrical output. The direction of energy conversion reverses, but losses remain.

Where the losses go

  • Copper losses: I²R heating in windings.
  • Core losses: hysteresis and eddy currents.
  • Mechanical losses: friction and windage.
  • Stray/load losses: additional losses that depend on the machine.

Useful checks

Motor slip is normally positive when an induction motor runs below synchronous speed in motoring mode. A transformer turns-ratio calculation is ideal: winding resistance, leakage flux, core loss, and regulation are not included.
