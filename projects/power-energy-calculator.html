Three useful power formulas

P = V × I

Use voltage and current when both are known.

P = I² × R

Use current and resistance.

P = V² / R

Energy: E = P × t. Wh = W × hours; kWh = Wh ÷ 1,000. For a simple resistor, Ohm's law requires V = I × R. A consistency check is only possible when all three electrical values are entered.

Read the result correctly

Power (W)

How quickly electrical energy is transferred or converted. One watt equals one joule per second.

Energy (Wh / kWh)

The accumulated use over time. A 100 W load operating for 5 hours uses 500 Wh, or 0.5 kWh.

Resistive DC loads

The three formulas are equivalent when Ohm's law applies and the values describe the same operating point.

AC caution

For AC circuits, real power can depend on RMS values, power factor, waveform, and phase. This calculator is not an AC power analyser.
